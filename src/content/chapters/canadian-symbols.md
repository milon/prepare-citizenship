Symbols are easy marks on the test if you learn a short list well.

## Flag, colours, and the Crown

The **National Flag** (1965) is red and white with a red **maple leaf**. Red and white are the official colours. The **Royal Union Flag** (Union Jack) still has a place as a symbol of membership in the Commonwealth and of allegiance to the Crown.

The **Crown** appears on police and military badges, on some coins, and in the language of government (“Crown land,” “Crown attorney”). It stands for the state as a continuing legal person, not for everyday politics.

## Parliament Hill and the Peace Tower

The **Parliament Buildings** in **Ottawa** are a national landmark. The **Peace Tower** holds a memorial chamber for the dead of the world wars and later conflicts. The **Library of Parliament** survived the 1916 fire that destroyed the original Centre Block.

## Animals, plants, and honours

- The **beaver** is a national animal emblem, tied to the fur trade
- The **maple leaf** is used far beyond the flag
- The **fleur-de-lis** is closely associated with French-Canadian heritage
- The **RCMP** (“Mounties”) is a widely recognized institutional symbol

The **Order of Canada** honours outstanding contribution to the country. Its motto is *Desiderantes meliorem patriam*—they desire a better country.

## Anthem, holidays, and sport

**O Canada** is the national anthem. **God Save the King** is the royal anthem. **Victoria Day** honours the Crown’s birthday for ceremonial purposes. **Remembrance Day** is November 11: wear a poppy, keep a minute of silence, and know **In Flanders Fields** by John McCrae.

**Hockey** is often treated as the national winter sport; **lacrosse** as the summer sport. You do not need to be a fan. You do need the pairing if the question appears.

## Provincial and territorial emblems

Each province and territory has a shield, floral emblem, and often an animal. For the test, national symbols come first; then know your own province’s capital and a couple of local identifiers (see the Regions pages).
