“Modern Canada” on the test means the decades after the Second World War: the welfare state, Quebec’s transformation, official bilingualism, and international partnerships.

## Prosperity and social programs

Post-war governments expanded public services that many Canadians now treat as part of the social contract:

- **Old Age Security**
- **Employment Insurance** (formerly unemployment insurance)
- **Canada Pension Plan / Quebec Pension Plan**
- Publicly funded **health care**, pioneered in Saskatchewan under Tommy Douglas and later adopted nationwide with federal support

These programs are provincial and federal in different mixes. Health care delivery is a provincial responsibility with national standards tied to federal funding.

## Quebec and official languages

The **Quiet Revolution** of the 1960s remade Quebec: a more secular public square, a stronger provincial state, and a confident French-speaking majority. Debates over Quebec’s place in Canada led to sovereignty referendums in **1980** and **1995**. Both remained in Canada.

The **Official Languages Act (1969)** made English and French the official languages of the Parliament and Government of Canada. Federal services must be available in both languages where numbers warrant.

## A more diverse country

Immigration policy moved away from racial preferences. People from Asia, the Caribbean, Africa, and Latin America arrived in large numbers. Cities changed fastest. **Multiculturalism** became official federal policy in 1971: people may keep cultural traditions while sharing civic laws and values.

## Canada in the world

Canada is a founding member of the **United Nations** and of **NATO**. With the United States it maintains **NORAD** for aerospace warning and control. Canada belongs to the **Commonwealth** and to **La Francophonie**. Peacekeeping became part of the national story, especially after the Suez Crisis, though Canada also fought in Korea and later in Afghanistan.

Work with the United States on trade is central. The study guide discusses North American free trade; the legal name of the current agreement has changed over time, but the idea—close economic integration with the United States and Mexico—has not.

## Remembering hard history

Modern Canada also includes apologies and redress: Japanese-Canadian internment, the Chinese head tax, residential schools, and the turning away of Jewish refugees in the 1930s. The test may still ask older “who/when” facts; a serious citizen should also know why those later reckonings happened.
