Four provinces face the Atlantic. They were among the earliest areas of European settlement in what became Canada, and three of them (Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, and the later-joining Prince Edward Island) sit at the heart of Confederation history. Newfoundland and Labrador joined in **1949**.

## Newfoundland and Labrador

- Capital: **St. John’s**, one of the oldest European-founded cities in North America
- Geography: the island of Newfoundland plus mainland Labrador
- Economy: historically fisheries; also energy, mining, and offshore oil
- Distinct time zone: Newfoundland time is a half-hour ahead of Atlantic time

## Prince Edward Island

- Capital: **Charlottetown**
- Smallest province by area and population
- Hosted the **1864 Charlottetown Conference**, a starting point for Confederation
- Economy: agriculture (including potatoes), tourism, fisheries

## Nova Scotia

- Capital: **Halifax**, the largest Atlantic city and a major port
- Strong British military and naval history; also Scottish, Acadian, and Black Loyalist heritage
- Economy: public services, the port, shipbuilding, fisheries, tourism

## New Brunswick

- Capital: **Fredericton**
- The only officially **bilingual** province
- Large **Acadian** population, especially in the north and east
- Economy: forestry, mining, energy, agriculture, and public administration

When a question says “Atlantic Canada,” it means these four provinces together—not “east of Ottawa.”
