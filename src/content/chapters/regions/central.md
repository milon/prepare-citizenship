**Ontario** and **Quebec** contain a majority of Canadians. They were Upper and Lower Canada, then the Province of Canada, then two of the four original provinces in 1867.

## Quebec

- Capital: **Quebec City**, founded in 1608
- Largest city: **Montreal**
- French is the official language of the province; a distinct legal tradition (civil law) governs private disputes
- Hydroelectric power, aerospace, culture, and a large services economy
- The **St. Lawrence** is the historic highway into the continent

Know the difference between Quebec City (capital) and Montreal (metropolis). Both appear as distractors on tests.

## Ontario

- Capital: **Toronto**, Canada’s largest city and a financial centre
- **Ottawa**, the national capital, is also in Ontario
- The **National Capital Region** includes Gatineau, Quebec, across the river
- Manufacturing in the south (including the auto sector), plus services, agriculture, and mining on the Shield
- The Great Lakes and the Canadian Shield dominate the map

## Why “central” is a test word

In the official study guide, “Central Canada” is Quebec and Ontario—not “the Prairies,” and not “everything that is not the coast.” Population, manufacturing, and federal politics have long been concentrated here, which is why regional fairness is a recurring theme in Canadian public life.
