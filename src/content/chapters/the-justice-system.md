Canada is governed by the **rule of law**: everyone, including the Prime Minister and the police, is subject to the law. Courts are independent of Cabinet and of Parliament.

## Criminal and civil law

- **Criminal law** is mainly federal. The government (the Crown) prosecutes offences such as theft or assault. The standard of proof is **beyond a reasonable doubt**. The accused is **presumed innocent**.
- **Civil law** covers disputes between people or organizations (contracts, family property, injuries). The standard is lower: balance of probabilities.

Quebec uses a **civil-law** tradition for private law, inherited from France. Other provinces use the **common-law** tradition inherited from England. Criminal law is common-law-based across the country, in federal statute.

## Courts

A typical path:

- **Provincial/territorial courts** — most criminal trials, family and youth matters in many places
- **Superior courts** — serious criminal trials and major civil cases
- **Courts of appeal**
- **Supreme Court of Canada** — the final court of appeal, with nine judges, including the Chief Justice

There are also federal courts for certain federal matters. Military justice and some regulatory tribunals sit beside this system.

Judges are appointed, not elected. They can be removed only for serious cause, through a demanding process. That independence is the point.

## Police and legal rights

Police services exist at municipal, provincial, and federal levels. The **RCMP** is the federal force and also contracts as the provincial police in much of the country.

The Charter protects you when dealing with the state:

- Unreasonable **search and seizure** is forbidden; a **warrant** is often required
- You have the right to **counsel** on arrest or detention
- You cannot be forced to testify against yourself
- You have the right to a **fair and public** hearing before an independent court
- **Habeas corpus** lets a court test whether detention is lawful

Police do not make the law and do not decide guilt. Courts do.

## Getting help

Legal aid, duty counsel, and community clinics exist because rights on paper mean little if you cannot reach a lawyer. If you are arrested, ask to speak to counsel and do not guess your way through a police interview.
