This chapter is the longest stretch of facts on the test. Focus on turning points, names, and dates—not on memorizing every battle.

## First peoples and early contact

Indigenous nations had trade, diplomacy, and war long before Europeans arrived. Norse visitors reached the Atlantic around the year 1000. Lasting European presence began with French and later British exploration, fisheries, and the **fur trade**. The **Hudson’s Bay Company** (1670) became a major commercial power in the northwest.

## New France and British North America

Samuel de Champlain founded **Quebec City in 1608**. New France grew along the St. Lawrence. In 1759, the British defeated the French on the Plains of Abraham. France ceded its Canadian colonies in 1763.

The **Quebec Act (1774)** protected French civil law and Catholicism. After the American Revolution, **Loyalists** moved north. The **Constitutional Act of 1791** created Upper Canada (Ontario) and Lower Canada (Quebec).

The **War of 1812** saw American invasions of British North America. The colonies remained British. The **Underground Railroad** later brought many enslaved people to freedom in Canada.

## Responsible government and Confederation

Reformers fought for **responsible government**—a Cabinet that must keep the confidence of an elected assembly. It was achieved in Nova Scotia in 1847–49 and then in the Province of Canada.

On **July 1, 1867**, the **British North America Act** created the Dominion of Canada. The first four provinces were **Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick**. **Sir John A. Macdonald** became the first Prime Minister. **Sir George-Étienne Cartier** was a leading Quebec partner in the project.

Talks at **Charlottetown** and **Quebec** in 1864 set the terms. The new country was a federation: some powers for Ottawa, some for the provinces.

## Building a continental country

Manitoba (1870), British Columbia (1871), Prince Edward Island (1873), and later the Prairie provinces and Newfoundland (1949) joined. The **Canadian Pacific Railway** linked British Columbia to the east. The **North-West Mounted Police**, ancestor of the RCMP, was created to bring Canadian authority to the Plains.

This expansion came with conflict, including the **Red River** and **North-West** resistances led by **Louis Riel**, and with policies that dispossessed Indigenous peoples.

## From world wars to a modern constitution

Canada entered the **First World War** in 1914 as part of the British Empire. **Vimy Ridge (1917)** became a national symbol of the Canadian Corps. **Women** gained the federal vote in stages around 1917–1918 (status Indian women much later).

In the **Second World War**, Canada fought from 1939, including at Dieppe, in Italy, on D-Day in Normandy, and in the liberation of the Netherlands. At home, Japanese Canadians were interned—now recognized as a grave injustice.

The **Constitution was patriated in 1982**, with the Charter. Quebec did not sign that package; later attempts at amendment failed, but Quebec remains a province of Canada.
